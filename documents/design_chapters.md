# Chapter design

Chapters (unsplittable sequences of scenes; see `design.md`, "Vocabulary") as data that can be assigned to any place meeting their requirements, so they can be shuffled and NPC talk can be written once against final interfaces. Cutscene machinery is in `design_scenes.md`; city layout and hinters are in `design_city_npcs.md`.

## Chapters

- Each special location has a hardcoded, thin chapter.
- A number of city chapters are authored; each city randomly selects one.
- Chapters are modeled after Pokémon TV-show episodes. Ideally the player meets each gym leader during a chapter before battling them.
- Each chapter is linear and cannot be split. Each stage has ~2 NPC hints of the form "I heard something is happening at [city/location]", ~2 of the form "I saw something happening at [building]", plus story-state texts.

Initial chapter content comes from Emerald (`Emerald.md`). More follow from Red/Blue/Yellow, then other games. In the far future, more chapters are modeled after TV show episodes.

## Chapter abstraction

Goal: city and special-location chapters become data that can be assigned to any place that meets their requirements, so chapters can be shuffled (stage 5) and NPC talk can be written once against the final interfaces. Shuffling is done when, for some seed, two city-bucket chapters whose role and progression requirements both hosts meet are swapped, both are completable, and NPCs in both cities hint correctly.

### Scope

Only chapter beats need converting; trainer text, item pickups, trainer-defeated flags and NPC-hide flags stay as they are. The lock/key entries in `Emerald_walkthrough.md` fall into buckets:

- Main arc (rival, Aqua/Magma, Wally, and any storyline spanning several counties, such as Devon Goods): stays on Emerald's vars behind the adapter until the arc cutscenes are generalized (Milestone 3). A chapter takes place entirely within a city and the starts of its adjacent routes, so a cross-county storyline is main arc (owned by its first county) or is split into per-city chapters.
- City chapter and special-location chapter: converted, and the only buckets needed to unblock chapter shuffling.
- Gate (an HM, key item or blocking NPC that gates progress): one mechanism, a lock with a key.
- Gym: a city chapter in the end state (the gym leader is met during the chapter, as in the anime and Black/White). Temporarily a separate fixed beat per city, so the Emerald story can be abstracted first.

### Placement and requirements

- The end state picks a city's chapter early in city generation, so the city knows which story buildings to place.
- Each chapter declares requirements on the cities or locations that may host it: required buildings (by role, e.g. gym, shop, house with an NPC), required HMs, biome, and terrain (mountain, coast, island). These requirements are expected to stay long term.
- Progression requirements: the keys and badges a chapter consumes (Norman needs 4 badges; Petalburg Woods needs Cut), and the keys it produces. Keys a chapter produces may only open locks relative to its host (its outbound routes); between counties those locks are planner unlock steps, ordered by main-story-index (a key's index is below its lock's), so the rule holds after a move. A key that opens a fixed lock elsewhere (Cut opens Petalburg Woods and Rusturf) is a gate, not a chapter output. Consumed requirements are either relative to the host (the lower neighbor's chapter is done) or counted (badges >= N, so the host's step must come after the Nth gym step). A chapter may move only to a host where these hold, which is what makes a swapped chapter provably completable.
- In the hardcoded stage, buildings are fixed, so a chapter is assigned only to a city whose existing buildings satisfy its requirements, and chapters address buildings by role, not by map.
- NPC hosting: each host building keeps a few placeholder object events whose graphics come from a var (`OBJ_EVENT_GFX_VAR_0`, `VAR_OBJ_GFX_ID_0`..`F`), a generic script, and a temp hide flag (counts and spawning: "Building roles and NPC spawning"); a chapter fills them by setting the vars and flags in the host map's on-transition script. These vars are shared (the rival graphics, battle frontier and Route 117 already set them), so they are never persistent state. This is cheaper than spawning NPCs at runtime, which is kept for cutscene beats (an NPC running up to the player) via the generic runner.

### Interfaces

Defined for the end state, not for Emerald, so NPC talk never needs redesign:

- Chapter descriptor: linear stages; per stage the hint texts, story-state texts and trigger place (by role) (hints are the six distance-by-state entries of "City planning"); the requirements above; rewards; and the key/lock it produces. A chapter that unlocks a location describes its output as a key for the gates on the routes into that location, not as "unlock X". A chapter supplies its own blocker scripts (the person who stands in the way, with texts) for now.
- Stage accessor: reads and writes a chapter's stage by slot (the county's reserved vars, see "Story state storage" (design_story.md)), never by chapter identity, so moving a chapter moves no state. A chapter that is a main-plot step has no state of its own: not started or current comes from the main-story-index (see "Main plot planner" (design_story.md)), and done or partly done from the county's chain position (see "County chain"). Chapters internal to a county use that county's reserved vars. Callers cannot tell the difference. The main-story-index is not maintained until the main arc is ported (Milestone 3), so until then main-line stages come from the adapter over Emerald's vars.
- Built so far (`include/util/chapter.h`, `src/util/chapter.c`, hosts in `src/data/region/emerald_chapters.h`, tests in `test/chapter.c`): `ChapterDesc` (stages with the six hint entries relative to the stage, beat anchor, extra roles of a multi-building cutscene, hinter count and roles, a hint count, actors with their stage ranges, done-predicate for adapter stages; required roles, minimum badges, required keys and the produced key with its production stage), `GetChapterStage`/`SetChapterStage` by (county, slot) over the county slice (var 0 of a slice is the county chain position, `include/util/county_chain.h` with `ResolveChainPosition`, `IsChainMainSlotDone` and `DidChainJustFinishMainSlot` as pure functions and Get/Set/AdvanceCountyChainPosition over the var; vars 1-3 are slots; only counties whose slice is free of legacy vars can host), `GetCountySlotHint` as the NPC-talk query (distance plus a per-NPC selector, NULL means generic text), and the script specials `GetCurrentChapterStage`/`SetCurrentChapterStage`/`IsCurrentChapterAvailable` (VAR_0x8004 = slot, county from the current map's MAPSEC, a city for now). Rustboro's Cutter's House (county (0,1), slot 0, one stage per HM given or not, anchored on the generic house), the Lilycove Museum exhibit hall (county (4,0), slot 0), Rustboro's fossil revival (slot 1: idle, reviving, ready; anchored on the service role at the Devon researcher; the Claw-or-Root choice is `FLAG_FOSSIL_IS_ANORITH`, and the stage returns to idle on receipt so the other fossil can follow), the Lilycove Pokéblock Case (slot 1, anchored on the new `BUILDING_CONTEST_HALL` role; the script still sets `FLAG_RECEIVED_POKEBLOCK_CASE`, no hinters yet) and Mossdeep's Super Rod fisherman (county (5,1), slot 0, the generic house; the first port whose beat is a scene: his talk script checks the stage and runs `SCENE_MOSSDEEP_SUPER_ROD`, whose `advance` writes the slot var), Littleroot's Save Birch (slot 1) and Moving in (slot 0, anchored on the home) and Oldale's Rival and Pokédex (slot 0, anchored on the Oldale road) are ported; vanilla scripts advance the two tutorial chapters. Adapter chapters reuse `GetChapterStage` and are read-only. A stage's hint entries describe that stage as a beat: before, current or after depending on whether the chapter is below, at or above it, so an NPC may recall any passed beat (`GetCountySlotHintAbout`). The last stage's close-after entry is never reached and may be empty. The consumers of requirements and keys are described under "Interface rules from paper tests".
- NPC-talk query: reads only descriptor, accessor and placement, and returns the text to say. It cannot tell a ported chapter from an adapter-backed one.
- Adapter: lets an unported Emerald chapter answer the accessor from its old vars (e.g. Petalburg gym "open" is `VAR_PETALBURG_GYM_STATE >= 6`). It is read-only, so NPCs can hint at unported chapters, but only ported chapters can be shuffled. Chapters are ported one at a time, each leaving `make check` green. The main-line half exists: `PlotGetDesc` (a done-predicate and a blocking or reported-only class per get), `GetPlotListStage(index)` (done and blocking masks over a list's gets) and `GetMainStoryIndex()` (the first list not done, scanning from 0) in `include/util/plot_stage.h`, `src/util/plot_stage.c`, with Emerald's predicates in `src/data/region/emerald_plot_gets.h` and tests in `test/region_plot_stage.c`. Only gets that vanilla forces are blocking, since a skippable blocking get would freeze the index; the rest are reported only (Emerald.md, "Step predicates for the read-only adapter").

### Text references

The script engine has no field-path variables. Text holds only the placeholders `{STR_VAR_1}`-`{STR_VAR_3}` (`gStringVar1-3`), a few fixed ones (`{PLAYER}`, `{RIVAL}`, `{AQUA}`...) expanded by `StringExpandPlaceholders` (`src/util/string_util.c`), and values that scripts put into the string vars with `buffer*` commands or a `special` function.

Authored text uses story references such as `$currentCity.gym.leader.name`, `$currentCity.nearbySmallCity.name`, `$stepCounty.city.theme.motto` or `$currentCity.pkmnCenter.nearBuildingName`. A reference is an anchor plus a path through the generated region data. A build tool turns each reference in a template into a compact reference id, and a single talk special expands the whole template in C into `gStringVar4` and shows it itself with `ShowFieldMessageFromBuffer` (not via the `message` command, which expands its source string into `gStringVar4` and would overlap it); this avoids the three-reference limit of `{STR_VAR_1}`-`{STR_VAR_3}`. Chapters are limited to a single county, so anchors are the speaker's own place (the NPC's city and building, for `$currentCity.pkmnCenter...`-style random talk), the chapter's own place, and, for hints about the main story, the responsible county of the current step (`$stepCounty`).

### Interface rules from paper tests

Checked against Petalburg gym/Surf, Devon Goods and Granite Cave/Flash in Emerald's scripts. The format absorbs all three with these rules, none needing a special case:

- Derived stages: a stage may be computed from requirements rather than stored. Petalburg gym's "locked, N badges short" and "ready" (`VAR_PETALBURG_GYM_STATE` 2-6) is just badges held, so a ported gym stores no counter and the other gyms stop writing to it (Emerald's four `addvar` calls plus `Common_EventScript_ReadyPetalburgGymForBattle` disappear). Badges held is the popcount of the badge flags (`GetBadgeCount`), not "gyms 1-4". A derived chapter is an adapter chapter whose stage predicates use `STORY_PRED_BADGES_GE`.
- Parameterized hints: a stage's `hintCount` names a number from a counted requirement (`CHAPTER_COUNT_BADGES_SHORT`: badges still missing for `minBadges`). The hint special writes it into `{STR_VAR_1}`, which the template uses ("{STR_VAR_1} more badges").
- Stage predicates: an adapter-backed chapter declares its stages as an ordered list of flag or var predicates, highest first, and the first true one is the stage. Devon Goods is DELIVERED, then RETURNED, then RECOVERED, then STOLEN, else not started. `FLAG_DEVON_GOODS_STOLEN` is cleared when RECOVERED is set, so the order, not set-ness, defines the stage. `FLAG_DOCK_REJECTED_DEVON_GOODS` is a side branch that changes only the Slateport shipyard talk, not the stage.
- One chapter, several buildings: a stage's trigger place is a building role, and a cutscene may walk across maps. Norman's defeat triggers a walk to Wally's house (`VAR_PETALBURG_CITY_STATE` 4) where the dad hands over Surf, so the Petalburg gym host needs a "house with an NPC" role beside the gym, and the cutscene runner crosses both. Emerald keeps this beat in a second var (`VAR_PETALBURG_CITY_STATE`, six values); ported, it is stages 3-5 of the one gym chapter. Each stage anchors one building (`anchor.role`) and may list further buildings its cutscene visits in `extraRoles`; the host must have every role (`GetChapterNeededRoles`).
- Keys: an HM key is a pair (HM given, badge held), and field-move checks read fixed badge flags (`src/field/field_move.c`), so shuffling which gym unlocks which HM must also change those. A key's production is a stage's reward, not a flag a gate reads directly: a descriptor's `producedKey` becomes held once the chapter reaches `producedKeyStage` (`IsMcGuffinProducedBy`), and `requiredKeys` must all be held before the chapter is available (`IsChapterAvailable`, with the badge count). A key is named relative to the host (`CHAPTER_KEY_HOST_POOL`, an index into the host county's McGuffin pool, so a moved chapter moves its key) or as a planner item (`CHAPTER_KEY_ITEM`, which other hosts may consume). `CheckChapterPlacement` is the static test for a move: the city has the roles, a chapter giving a main-plot get comes after the gym its badges wait for (any other waits at runtime), and every consumed key has a producer in an earlier county, or an earlier slot of the same one. `IsCurrentChapterAvailable` is the script special.
- Cross-host effects: a chapter's completion may write another host's state. Beating Norman hides Wattson in his gym and shows him in Mauville, which is what starts New Mauville. That is a produced key ("gym 5 done") consumed by New Mauville's requirements, not a flag write. The gym itself is the producer: it emits the fifth badge, so its input is unspecified but its output is fixed, which no ordinary chapter has. An ordinary lock is an obstacle right outside the next place (most often an NPC or a scripted scene), and the key is the previous chapter's outcome that clears it. Littleroot and Oldale chain this way: the twin blocks the exit (lock) until the player has met the rival (key; the code's `SAVE_BIRCH_TWIN_ASKS` is set by the rival meeting); Birch's chase on Route 101 blocks the route (lock) until the starter is chosen and the Zigzagoon beaten (key); the rival battle on Route 103 unlocks Birch's Pokédex talk (lock and key); and the Pokédex unlocks the sketch artist who blocks Oldale's west exit.
- Main-arc steps are hosted by counties, not locations: a county places its steps anywhere (city, route location, plain route) as long as the player can and must take them in order. The Devon Goods letter delivery happens to sit in Granite Cave's 1F side room (`FLAG_DELIVERED_STEVEN_LETTER`), but its host is the Dewford county.
- Devon Goods stays main arc: it spans Rustboro (theft, return), the Rusturf tunnel (recovery), Dewford and the Granite Cave (letter) and Slateport (delivery), and its recovery key opens a lock back in the first county, so it is a callback, not four per-city chapters.
- Dark caves: Emerald has no script check for Flash. Dark floors (Granite Cave B1F and B2F are `requires_flash`) only dim the screen, and the engine lets the player walk them unlit. Flash is made a hard key by limiting an unlit player to about 6 tiles from the entrance of a `requires_flash` map. Steven's room is a warp off the lit 1F, so the letter delivery never needs Flash.

### Building roles and NPC spawning

- Roles are the `BuildingRole` bit set (`region_city.h`), with the list in `Emerald.md` ("Building roles"); the generic house, which the Petalburg gym/Surf paper test needs beside the gym, is `BUILDING_HOUSE`. A chapter addresses buildings by role only.
- Placeholder NPC objects per host map: 2 for each building role, 3 for a gym, 1 for a service building (its own NPC is fixed), 4 for a city's outdoor map, none for routes. The 16 shared `VAR_OBJ_GFX_ID_*` vars are set by the host map's on-transition script, so the limit is per map, not per game; a city map already spends 1-2 of them on the rival, so 4 is well inside it. Placeholders hide through temp flags (cleared on map load), so they cost no saved flags.
- Runtime NPC spawning is needed only for cutscene actors (an NPC runs up to the player and off again), because a generated route or city has no object in the right place. The generic cutscene runner builds an object template at runtime and spawns it (`SpawnSpecialObjectEvent`, as the player avatar does); to be confirmed when the runner is written. Chapter NPCs inside buildings never spawn at runtime: placeholders cover them, and the rejected alternative below explains why.
- Route hint NPCs (Milestone 1) are not decided here.

### County planning and chapter placement

A county plans its chapters algorithmically from the seed, in this order, and stores none of it (like the main-story order):

1. Unique buildings: a coprime-stride deal picks the unique buildings a county has beyond its generic ones.
2. Attached chapters: the county looks at the main-story beats it hosts, its locations and its unique buildings, and takes the chapters attached to them (Pokémon Tower: Fuji held by Rocket, a Marowak, and a Silph Scope as a consumed key). An attached chapter is never shuffled, so its requirements hold by construction.
3. Generic chapters: a second coprime-stride deal picks from the generic pool. A candidate that fails `CheckChapterPlacement` for the county is skipped to the next stride step. The pool will be short once everything is randomized, so more are added from FRLG or custom.
4. McGuffins to provide: the county reads the main-story beats it hosts for the keys it must supply. The fallback NPC guarantees them: a generic one-role chapter whose first stage hints its requirement (badges, a key) and whose second gives the McGuffin. Completability then follows from construction.
5. Location order: the county reads which locations carry inter-county routes, and those counties' story indices, to order its locations.
6. Slot details: the county derives each chapter slot's details (route ends, building doorways) on demand.

### Plot kinds

- Location-tied (attached) chapters belong to a location or unique building and are never shuffled.
- City chapters are generic and dealt to cities by coprime stride.
- The main story is the cross-county plot: its steps are lists of gets hosted by counties, and the global main-story-index (a stored var, `4x + s` over counties and slots) says which step is current. Each county has 4 main-story slots, slot 1 usually being a callback hosted by another county.
- A subplot is an ordered sequence of related chapters telling one story (`design.md`, "Vocabulary"); it interleaves with other subplots and with the main story. Code has no subplot type yet: a county's chain stands in for one.
- Every plot is a sequence of stages. It may require an intro McGuffin, and maps stage ranges, including "before" and "after" pseudo-stages, to hint texts. Every chapter ends with a reward: usually a McGuffin, sometimes a rare item (rare candy, mushroom), sometimes only story advancement.

### County chain

A county fulfills its main-story slots through its own chapters, and its progress through them is the county's var 0. The county picks, per main-story slot, which chapters supply the slot's gets and the intro McGuffins those chapters need; any surplus chapters are optional. Var 0 counts through the concatenated stages of the chain's chapters, in slot order, skipping each chapter's "before" and "after" pseudo-stages, and the accessor resolves a value to (chapter, stage) by cumulative stage counts.

Example: slot 0 provides a Silph Scope and a rival battle; the county assigns Nugget Bridge (1 stage, the rival battle), Copycat Girl (1 stage, a Rocket key, the intro McGuffin of the next chapter) and the Rocket hideout (3 stages, the Silph Scope), so var 0 runs 0 Nugget Bridge, 1 doll to Copycat Girl, 2 enter the hideout, 3 elevator key, 4 defeat the boss and take the scope. Completing the slot's last stage also advances the global main-story-index to the next slot (the callback in another county). When that resolves, the index returns to this county's slot 2, and var 0 continues from 5 through the chapters assigned to slot 2, then slot 3, whose completion opens access to the next county and advances the index there. A chain entry that needs an optional chapter done consumes a host-pool key the optional chapter produces. Optional chapters keep their own stage in vars 1-3.

Chapters and stages differ only in granularity: stages of one chapter could be seen as small chapters. A chapter is immutable, while a subplot (an ordered sequence of chapters, such as this chain) is what interleaves with other subplots. So a sequence the walkthrough calls one chapter may be coded as two ordered by a McGuffin. Prefer smaller chapters, so the game can interleave subplots with each other.

Ordering a chain: by default a chapter has no declared key (`producedKey` unset): its key is its own completion, so it stays locked until the previous chapter in the chain completes and completing it unlocks the next. `IsCountySlotAvailable` implements this (`IsChainPredecessorComplete`): earlier counties count as done once the main-story-index has reached the county's slots (`index / 4 >= storyIdx`), and within the county the previous slot's chapter must have reached its last stage. `IsChapterAvailable` itself checks only badges and declared keys. Most chapters are like this. An explicit key is only for the rare fixed ones. A chapter may have a fixed input (a key it requires) and a fixed output (a key others consume), and most have neither. Gyms have no input (the badge count is not a key) and a fixed output, so they sit last in their slot's chain, and a gym is easy to fork out of the sequence later: it runs in parallel with the slot's other chapters, and some lock in the county requires it done before the next county opens.

## Alternatives considered

### Chapters bound to their original maps (shuffling only by swapping maps)

Keep each chapter's NPCs and events in its Emerald maps, and shuffle by swapping whole cities.

Pros:
- No new runtime NPC machinery.

Cons:
- Chapters cannot move between cities, which is what stage 5 needs.
- Conflicts with procedural buildings, where maps are not fixed.
